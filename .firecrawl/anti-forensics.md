## Abstract

With the increasing use of digital technology, people are relying on digital data for various tasks. However, this reliance has led to a rise in cyberattacks. To combat these threats, digital forensic departments are tasked with investigating such incidents. Yet, attackers continuously develop new methods to conceal their actions. These actions are considered anti-forensics, where attackers try to hamper the digital forensic investigation process by manipulating or deleting evidence to hide their tracks of malicious activities or to mislead the forensic process. Therefore, implementing effective countermeasures is essential to stop these attackers. This has resulted in a systematic review of existing literature in this domain to comprehensively understand anti-forensic techniques, evaluate current countermeasures, and identify areas for further research and development. This paper aimed to provide an overview of the anti-forensic tactics in cybercrime investigations along with their corresponding countermeasures. Digital forensics experts face many challenges in their investigations as sophisticated cyber criminals hide their tracks. The limitations of current digital forensics techniques and tools were explored, and improvements and countermeasures were suggested. Our paper aims to enhance the understanding of the digital forensics field and its associated challenges. The findings of this research are valuable as they identify key gaps in current cybersecurity practices, and these have been highlighted in the knowledge gap section.

## 1 Introduction

Digital forensics is increasingly important as the use of digital devices has become widespread today. Digital forensics is a field that involves the collection, preservation, and analysis of digital data for use as evidence in cybercrime investigations \[ [1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR1 "Alenezi, A.M.: Digital and cloud forensic challenges. arXiv preprint arXiv:2305.03059. (2023)")\]. Digital Forensics is “The use of scientifically derived and proven methods toward the preservation, collection, validation, identification, analysis, interpretation, documentation, and presentation of digital evidence derived from digital sources to facilitate or further the reconstruction of events found to be criminal, or helping to anticipate unauthorized actions shown to be disruptive to planned operations” \[ [2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR2 "Mothi, D., Janicke, H., Wagner, I.: A novel principle to validate digital forensic models. Forensic Sci. International: Digit. Invest. 33, 200904 (2020)")\]. A forensic investigator handles each case, maintaining set protocols for evidence collection, acquisition, examination, and reporting while maintaining a chain of custody and preserving evidence integrity \[ [3](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR3 "Javed, A.R., Ahmed, W., Alazab, M., Jalil, Z., Kifayat, K., Gadekallu, T.R.: A comprehensive survey on computer forensics: State-of-the-art, tools, techniques, challenges, and future directions. IEEE Access. 10, 11065–11089 (2022)")\].

Anti-forensics (AF) can delay the investigation or prevent the investigation from taking place by deleting evidence. Anti-forensics research encompasses the study of techniques and tools employed by cybercriminals to hinder or manipulate digital forensic investigations \[ [4](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR4 "Majed, H., Noura, H.N., Chehab, A.: Overview of digital forensics and anti-forensics techniques. In 2020 8th International Symposium on Digital Forensics and Security (ISDFS) (pp. 1–5). IEEE. (2020), June"), [5](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR5 "Dahbur, K., Mohammad, B.: The anti-forensics challenge. In Proceedings of the 2011 International Conference on Intelligent Semantic Web-Services and Applications (pp. 1–7). (2011), April"), [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR6 "Hosgor, E.C.: Detection and mitigation of anti-forensics. Int. J. Comput. Sci. Inform. Secur. (IJCSIS), 18(12). (2020)"), [7](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR7 "Al-Mousa, M.R., Sweerky, N.A., Samara, G., Alghanim, M., Hussein, A.S.I., Qadoumi, B.: General countermeasures of Anti-Forensics categories. In: 2021 Global Congress on Electrical Engineering (GC-ElecEng), pp. 5–10. IEEE (2021)"), [8](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR8 "Hammoud, A.H.: Efficient Countermeasures against Anti-Forensics Techniques. (2022)")\]. These techniques target the availability, usefulness, and scientific validity of digital evidence, thereby complicating or preventing the forensic analysis process \[ [4](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR4 "Majed, H., Noura, H.N., Chehab, A.: Overview of digital forensics and anti-forensics techniques. In 2020 8th International Symposium on Digital Forensics and Security (ISDFS) (pp. 1–5). IEEE. (2020), June")\]. AF methods aim to frustrate forensic efforts at all stages, including evidence acquisition, examination, analysis, and reporting. Malicious actors try to attack the three main constraints of time, cost, and resources of forensic investigations \[ [5](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR5 "Dahbur, K., Mohammad, B.: The anti-forensics challenge. In Proceedings of the 2011 International Conference on Intelligent Semantic Web-Services and Applications (pp. 1–7). (2011), April")\]. Modern cybersecurity uses both preventive measures and response actions to stay resilient and support investigations. This research contributes to the field by exploring strategies aimed at preventing cyber criminals from covering their tracks in the first place and addressing cybersecurity challenges to significantly enhance overall resilience.

As digital technologies continue to advance, so do the tactics employed by cybercriminals to conceal their activities and evade detection. Anti-forensic (AF) techniques have emerged as formidable adversaries in the realm of cybercrime investigations, posing significant challenges to the effectiveness of digital forensic practices. These techniques encompass a wide array of methods designed to obfuscate, alter, or destroy digital evidence, thereby impeding investigative efforts and undermining the integrity of forensic analyses. In addition to external cyber threats, this research project also addresses the challenge of insider threats, where individuals within an organization intentionally or unintentionally cover their tracks to conceal illicit activities. By developing techniques to detect and mitigate insider threats, this research enhances the overall resilience of organizations against both internal and external cyber threats, representing a novel contribution to cybersecurity practice.

By developing strategies and technologies to prevent cyber criminals from hiding their tracks effectively, this research contributes to enhancing overall cyber resilience. Organizations will be better equipped to detect and respond to cyber threats in real-time, minimizing the impact of attacks and reducing the likelihood of successful breaches. This proactive stance towards cyber defence is a significant advancement in cybersecurity practices. The cybersecurity threat landscape is constantly evolving, with cyber criminals continuously adapting their tactics and techniques to evade detection. This research addresses this challenge by reviewing, analysing, and presenting effective countermeasures to prevent track-covering attempts by highlighting best practices, tools, and recommendations from academic research. This work supports organizations in taking a more proactive and adaptive approach to digital forensics and cybersecurity.

## 2 Background

The research topic centres on countering anti-forensic (AF) techniques in cybercrime investigations. As digital technologies become increasingly integrated into various aspects of daily life, the threats have expanded, leading to a rise in cyberattacks. Traditional digital forensic methods face challenges due to the AF tactics. Anti-forensics, a term referring to the efforts of attackers to hinder or manipulate digital forensic investigations, has emerged as an obstacle for digital forensic practitioners. These tactics encompass a range of strategies aimed at tampering with evidence, disrupting forensic processes, and evading detection by forensic analysts \[ [2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR2 "Mothi, D., Janicke, H., Wagner, I.: A novel principle to validate digital forensic models. Forensic Sci. International: Digit. Invest. 33, 200904 (2020)")\]. This has initiated a critical need for robust countermeasures to enhance the integrity and effectiveness of digital forensic investigations \[ [2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR2 "Mothi, D., Janicke, H., Wagner, I.: A novel principle to validate digital forensic models. Forensic Sci. International: Digit. Invest. 33, 200904 (2020)")\]. Additionally, literature highlights the essential role of understanding digital forensics and its associated challenges in combating cybercrime effectively. Studies emphasize the importance of developing comprehensive methodologies and tools to address the evolving nature of cyber threats and ensure the reliability of forensic processes \[ [3](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR3 "Javed, A.R., Ahmed, W., Alazab, M., Jalil, Z., Kifayat, K., Gadekallu, T.R.: A comprehensive survey on computer forensics: State-of-the-art, tools, techniques, challenges, and future directions. IEEE Access. 10, 19469–19480 (2022)")\].

The impact of anti-forensic attacks on digital forensic investigations is significant, necessitating the development and implementation of effective countermeasures. Academic literature has explored various approaches to mitigate the impact of AF, including the enhancement of forensic methodologies, the deployment of advanced forensic tools, and the adoption of proactive forensic strategies \[ [10](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR10 "Tanner, A., Dancer, F.C., Hall, J., Parker, N., Bishop, R., McBride, T.: The Need for Proactive Digital Forensics in Addressing Critical Infrastructure Cyber Attacks. (2019)")\]. These countermeasures are meant to make digital forensic processes stronger against AF tactics. But even with these improvements, there are still challenges. This shows that we need to keep coming up with new ideas and working together in the digital forensic field to deal with anti-forensic challenges \[ [12](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR12 "Al-Asli, M., Ghaleb, T.A.: Review of signature-based techniques in antivirus products. The objective of this paper is to analyse the existing research landscape, identify key issues and shortcomings, and propose strategies for improving the effectiveness of digital forensics in combating AF techniques. Through a comprehensive review of relevant literature, we aim to provide insights into the challenges faced by digital forensic practitioners and suggest avenues for future research to bridge the identified gaps. Historically, cybercriminals have been adept at covering their tracks, posing significant challenges to digital forensics investigators. Thus, it is imperative to understand the tactics these cybercriminals use to hide. This research addresses a critical gap in forensic capabilities. This proactive approach represents a shift from reactive investigation to proactive prevention, which is relatively novel in the field.

This research is important because it addresses the field of cybersecurity and digital forensics by focusing on countering anti-forensic techniques. By conducting a systematic literature review and analysing existing literature, this research aims to contribute knowledge to the field of anti-forensics, its impact on digital forensics, and the effectiveness of current countermeasures. The results of this study will be useful to researchers, forensic investigators, and practitioners to understand the challenges posed by AF techniques and help the development of more effective and robust countermeasures and strategies to enhance digital forensic processes against cyber threats.

It also described both manual and advanced search techniques using keywords, Boolean operators, and different databases like Scopus and Google Scholar. Techniques like forward and backward tracking were used to identify important papers that may not have appeared in the initial search results. The same approach was followed in this review to ensure a comprehensive and unbiased selection of literature. Following this established approach helped maintain clarity, consistency, and credibility in our review process. A detailed explanation of the steps followed is provided in the next section.

### 4.1 The methodological process

The systematic literature review was conducted using a structured and rigorous methodology. In Step 1, relevant articles were retrieved from multiple academic databases, including IEEE Xplore, ACM Digital Library, Web of Science, Scopus, Springer, ProQuest, and ScienceDirect. A combination of keywords such as _“Digital Forensics_, _” “Anti-Forensics_, _” “Anti-Anti-Forensics_, _” “Counter Anti-Forensics_, _”_ and _“Forensics Challenges”_ was used, applying both manual and advanced search techniques with Boolean operators to ensure comprehensive coverage.

In Step 2, titles and abstracts of the retrieved articles were screened for relevance based on predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria. Only papers directly aligned with the objectives of this review were selected for further analysis.

Step 3 involved a quality assessment of the shortlisted papers. This was based on criteria such as the credibility of the publication source, the reputation of the authors, methodological soundness, and the relevance of findings. Additionally, citation metrics (e.g., from Google Scholar or Scopus) were collected for each paper to help identify highly influential and authoritative studies within the domain. This ensured that the most impactful literature contributed significantly to the review findings.

In Step 4, relevant data were extracted from the finalized set of articles to derive key insights, identify research gaps, and classify the contributions across various themes related to digital forensics and anti-forensics.

### 5.1 Anti-forensic techniques

The anti-forensics tools (AFT) and techniques are used to target the availability and usefulness of digital evidence by performing some actions such as altering, disrupting, and destroying the scientific validity of evidence. Anti-forensic techniques aim to avoid detection of malicious actions, disrupt information collection, increase investigation time, decrease the validity of forensic reports, reveal the presence of forensic tools, and clear evidence of anti-forensic tool existence \[ [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR6 "Hosgor, E.C.: Detection and mitigation of anti-forensics. Int. J. Comput. Sci. Inform. Secur. (IJCSIS), 18(12). (2020)")\]. These methods foil forensic processes by destroying, hiding, or modifying digital evidence, thereby complicating the detection of cybercrimes \[ [7](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR7 "Al-Mousa, M.R., Sweerky, N.A., Samara, G., Alghanim, M., Hussein, A.S.I., Qadoumi, B.: General countermeasures of Anti-Forensics categories. In: 2021 Global Congress on Electrical Engineering (GC-ElecEng), pp. 5–10. IEEE (2021)")\]. Overall, anti-forensics poses a significant challenge to digital forensic investigations and investigators \[ [8](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR8 "Hammoud, A.H.: Efficient Countermeasures against Anti-Forensics Techniques. (2022)")\]. AFT is divided into multiple categories according to the purpose of usage and the type of attacks performed.

The Anti-Forensics (AF) techniques can be categorized into four main categories as outlined by \[ [4](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR4 "Majed, H., Noura, H.N., Chehab, A.: Overview of digital forensics and anti-forensics techniques. In 2020 8th International Symposium on Digital Forensics and Security (ISDFS) (pp. 1–5). IEEE. (2020), June")\]. Multiple papers \[ [5](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR5 "Dahbur, K., Mohammad, B.: The anti-forensics challenge. In Proceedings of the 2011 International Conference on Intelligent Semantic Web-Services and Applications (pp. 1–7). (2011), April"), [7](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR7 "Al-Mousa, M.R., Sweerky, N.A., Samara, G., Alghanim, M., Hussein, A.S.I., Qadoumi, B.: General countermeasures of Anti-Forensics categories. In: 2021 Global Congress on Electrical Engineering (GC-ElecEng), pp. 5–10. IEEE (2021)"), [8](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR8 "Hammoud, A.H.: Efficient Countermeasures against Anti-Forensics Techniques. (2022)"), [14](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR14 "Hausknecht, K., Gruičić, S.: Anti-computer forensics. In: 2017 40th International Convention on Information and Communication Technology, Electronics and Microelectronics (MIPRO), pp. 1233–1240. IEEE (2017, May)"), [15](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR15 "Abdullahi, Z.H., Sagarwal, N., Soni, M.: An Overview of Anti-forensic Techniques and their Impact on Digital Forensic Analysis. (2020)")\] discuss AF techniques using the same categorization:

### Artifact wiping

This technique involves completely erasing and destroying data to hinder forensic analysis. It includes various types such as file wiping, disk/partition wiping, disk degaussing, registry wiping, and metadata manipulation.

### Data hiding

Data hiding aims to conceal the existence of data on storage devices, making it challenging for forensic examiners to analyse. Techniques include steganography, data concealment, file system manipulation, hard disk manipulation, encryption, and network-based data hiding.

### Trail obfuscation

Also known as “counterfeiting,” this technique is employed to confuse and disorient investigations. Methods include log manipulation, IP address spoofing, P2P networking, and proxy servers.

### Attacks against forensic tools and processes

These attacks aim to mislead or divert examiners from accessing accurate information. Examples include encryption, steganography, secure deletion, timestamp manipulation, file signature or magic number manipulation, DDoS attacks, anti-reverse engineering techniques, and attacks against the integrity of investigating parties.

These techniques and methods are used by cybercriminals to evade detection and hinder the forensic investigation process.

#### 5.1.1 Artifact wiping

Artifact wiping is widely documented in the literature as one of the most common anti-forensic (AF) techniques, with prevalence supported by both qualitative and quantitative evidence. Multiple studies \[ [4](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR4 "Majed, H., Noura, H.N., Chehab, A.: Overview of digital forensics and anti-forensics techniques. In 2020 8th International Symposium on Digital Forensics and Security (ISDFS) (pp. 1–5). IEEE. (2020), June"), [5](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR5 "Dahbur, K., Mohammad, B.: The anti-forensics challenge. In Proceedings of the 2011 International Conference on Intelligent Semantic Web-Services and Applications (pp. 1–7). (2011), April"), [7](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR7 "Al-Mousa, M.R., Sweerky, N.A., Samara, G., Alghanim, M., Hussein, A.S.I., Qadoumi, B.: General countermeasures of Anti-Forensics categories. In: 2021 Global Congress on Electrical Engineering (GC-ElecEng), pp. 5–10. IEEE (2021)")\] note that a broad range of readily available tools — such as Eraser, File Shredder, Registry Cleaner, and SDelete — make implementation straightforward even for non-expert users. The ease of installation, availability as freeware, and ease of use and learn \[ [5](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR5 "Dahbur, K., Mohammad, B.: The anti-forensics challenge. In Proceedings of the 2011 International Conference on Intelligent Semantic Web-Services and Applications (pp. 1–7). (2011), April"), [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR6 "Hosgor, E.C.: Detection and mitigation of anti-forensics. Int. J. Comput. Sci. Inform. Secur. (IJCSIS), 18(12). (2020)")\] directly increase the likelihood of occurrence. Empirical data in \[ [5](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR5 "Dahbur, K., Mohammad, B.: The anti-forensics challenge. In Proceedings of the 2011 International Conference on Intelligent Semantic Web-Services and Applications (pp. 1–7). (2011), April")\] shows artifact wiping accounting for 26% of observed AF cases, the highest among surveyed techniques. Wiping is also simple to execute on modern HDDs and file systems \[ [14](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR14 "Hausknecht, K., Gruičić, S.: Anti-computer forensics. In: 2017 40th International Convention on Information and Communication Technology, Electronics and Microelectronics (MIPRO), pp. 1233–1240. IEEE (2017, May)")\] and can be automated by native system functions. Detection during or after execution is difficult: experiments in \[ [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR6 "Hosgor, E.C.: Detection and mitigation of anti-forensics. Int. J. Comput. Sci. Inform. Secur. (IJCSIS), 18(12). (2020)")\] show that, once wiping is completed, forensic file carving yields no recoverable content, with only traces of the wiping tool found in memory. Similarly \[ [7](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR7 "Al-Mousa, M.R., Sweerky, N.A., Samara, G., Alghanim, M., Hussein, A.S.I., Qadoumi, B.: General countermeasures of Anti-Forensics categories. In: 2021 Global Congress on Electrical Engineering (GC-ElecEng), pp. 5–10. IEEE (2021)")\], concludes that securely recovering eliminated files is effectively impossible. Given this combination of high tool availability, ease of use, and detection difficulty, the likelihood is rated High. In terms of severity, artifact wiping directly results in complete, irreversible data destruction \[ [5](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR5 "Dahbur, K., Mohammad, B.: The anti-forensics challenge. In Proceedings of the 2011 International Conference on Intelligent Semantic Web-Services and Applications (pp. 1–7). (2011), April"), [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR6 "Hosgor, E.C.: Detection and mitigation of anti-forensics. Int. J. Comput. Sci. Inform. Secur. (IJCSIS), 18(12). (2020)"), [7](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR7 "Al-Mousa, M.R., Sweerky, N.A., Samara, G., Alghanim, M., Hussein, A.S.I., Qadoumi, B.: General countermeasures of Anti-Forensics categories. In: 2021 Global Congress on Electrical Engineering (GC-ElecEng), pp. 5–10. IEEE (2021)")\], leaving investigators without critical evidence. As it removes artifacts entirely rather than merely obscuring them, the investigative impact is severe, often halting analysis or preventing case reconstruction. Therefore, severity is also rated High.

#### 5.1.2 Data hiding

Data hiding in anti-forensics encompasses techniques such as encryption, steganography, and memory-based hiding, each varying in ease of implementation and detectability. Many modern forensic tools can detect the presence of encryption or steganography \[ [14](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR14 "Hausknecht, K., Gruičić, S.: Anti-computer forensics. In: 2017 40th International Convention on Information and Communication Technology, Electronics and Microelectronics (MIPRO), pp. 1233–1240. IEEE (2017, May)")\], and commercial steganalysis tools are available to reveal hidden data \[ [5](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR5 "Dahbur, K., Mohammad, B.: The anti-forensics challenge. In Proceedings of the 2011 International Conference on Intelligent Semantic Web-Services and Applications (pp. 1–7). (2011), April")\]. However, detection does not guarantee access — without the correct key, decrypting strong encryption is computationally infeasible \[ [5](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR5 "Dahbur, K., Mohammad, B.: The anti-forensics challenge. In Proceedings of the 2011 International Conference on Intelligent Semantic Web-Services and Applications (pp. 1–7). (2011), April")\], especially with long key lengths. While memory-based hidden data retrieval is relatively easy to implement, as anti-forensic tools in main memory do not try to hide themselves very much \[ [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR6 "Hosgor, E.C.: Detection and mitigation of anti-forensics. Int. J. Comput. Sci. Inform. Secur. (IJCSIS), 18(12). (2020)")\], and detection of some communication channels (e.g., stunnel) is possible, actual data retrieval remains challenging \[ [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR6 "Hosgor, E.C.: Detection and mitigation of anti-forensics. Int. J. Comput. Sci. Inform. Secur. (IJCSIS), 18(12). (2020)")\].

Open-source solutions like TrueCrypt, PGP, and cryptographic file systems for Windows, Linux, and Mac OS are freely accessible \[ [7](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR7 "Al-Mousa, M.R., Sweerky, N.A., Samara, G., Alghanim, M., Hussein, A.S.I., Qadoumi, B.: General countermeasures of Anti-Forensics categories. In: 2021 Global Congress on Electrical Engineering (GC-ElecEng), pp. 5–10. IEEE (2021)")\]. Similarly, encrypted VPNs and anonymizing networks like TOR are widely deployed. However, these methods are rarely used since they are hard to implement and require a lot of time to decode \[ [14](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR14 "Hausknecht, K., Gruičić, S.: Anti-computer forensics. In: 2017 40th International Convention on Information and Communication Technology, Electronics and Microelectronics (MIPRO), pp. 1233–1240. IEEE (2017, May)")\]. Steganography is especially more complex to implement effectively on frequently accessed data \[ [14](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR14 "Hausknecht, K., Gruičić, S.: Anti-computer forensics. In: 2017 40th International Convention on Information and Communication Technology, Electronics and Microelectronics (MIPRO), pp. 1233–1240. IEEE (2017, May)")\], reducing its likelihood in practice. Studies show that while forensic suites such as FTK Imager, Autopsy, Bulk Extractor, and TSK can detect some hidden content (e.g., slack space strings), success varies across file systems \[ [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR6 "Hosgor, E.C.: Detection and mitigation of anti-forensics. Int. J. Comput. Sci. Inform. Secur. (IJCSIS), 18(12). (2020)")\]. Given this, the likelihood is rated Medium — tools and methods are readily available, but some sub-techniques (notably steganography) require skill and are less commonly observed due to complexity \[ [14](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR14 "Hausknecht, K., Gruičić, S.: Anti-computer forensics. In: 2017 40th International Convention on Information and Communication Technology, Electronics and Microelectronics (MIPRO), pp. 1233–1240. IEEE (2017, May)")\]. Detection is possible but often incomplete, particularly for encryption, where presence can be confirmed but content remains inaccessible without the key \[ [5](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR5 "Dahbur, K., Mohammad, B.: The anti-forensics challenge. In Proceedings of the 2011 International Conference on Intelligent Semantic Web-Services and Applications (pp. 1–7). (2011), April"), [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR6 "Hosgor, E.C.: Detection and mitigation of anti-forensics. Int. J. Comput. Sci. Inform. Secur. (IJCSIS), 18(12). (2020)")\].

For severity, the impact on investigations is significant: encryption can disrupt acquisition and halt subsequent forensic phases \[ [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR6 "Hosgor, E.C.: Detection and mitigation of anti-forensics. Int. J. Comput. Sci. Inform. Secur. (IJCSIS), 18(12). (2020)")\], encrypted network protocols can conceal entire streams of illegal data \[ [7](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR7 "Al-Mousa, M.R., Sweerky, N.A., Samara, G., Alghanim, M., Hussein, A.S.I., Qadoumi, B.: General countermeasures of Anti-Forensics categories. In: 2021 Global Congress on Electrical Engineering (GC-ElecEng), pp. 5–10. IEEE (2021)")\], and steganography can hide illegal or suspicious content inside normal-looking files \[ [5](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR5 "Dahbur, K., Mohammad, B.: The anti-forensics challenge. In Proceedings of the 2011 International Conference on Intelligent Semantic Web-Services and Applications (pp. 1–7). (2011), April")\]. When effective, these methods cause partial or total loss of accessible evidence, misdirect investigations, and consume substantial analysis time. Given these factors, severity is rated Medium to High.

#### 5.1.3 Trail obfuscation

Trail obfuscation techniques, including log manipulation, timestamp modification, IP spoofing, use of P2P networking, and proxy servers, are widely documented in the literature. Multiple studies \[ [4](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR4 "Majed, H., Noura, H.N., Chehab, A.: Overview of digital forensics and anti-forensics techniques. In 2020 8th International Symposium on Digital Forensics and Security (ISDFS) (pp. 1–5). IEEE. (2020), June"), [5](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR5 "Dahbur, K., Mohammad, B.: The anti-forensics challenge. In Proceedings of the 2011 International Conference on Intelligent Semantic Web-Services and Applications (pp. 1–7). (2011), April")\] identify readily available tools such as _Timestamp Modifier_ and _SKTimeStamp_ that make such manipulations straightforward, even for non-expert users. Academic research notes that misdirection can involve timestamp changes, file defragmentation, and log manipulation, and that deleted logs cannot be recovered if they were not forwarded \[ [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR6 "Hosgor, E.C.: Detection and mitigation of anti-forensics. Int. J. Comput. Sci. Inform. Secur. (IJCSIS), 18(12). (2020)")\]. Detection is described as difficult \[ [7](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR7 "Al-Mousa, M.R., Sweerky, N.A., Samara, G., Alghanim, M., Hussein, A.S.I., Qadoumi, B.: General countermeasures of Anti-Forensics categories. In: 2021 Global Congress on Electrical Engineering (GC-ElecEng), pp. 5–10. IEEE (2021)")\], with altered timestamps and logs often blending seamlessly into normal system activity. Given these factors, the likelihood is rated High. The combination of ease of implementation, widespread tool availability, and difficulty of detection, as consistently reported in the literature, directly increases the likelihood of occurrence.

In terms of severity, trail obfuscation can significantly mislead an investigation through data manipulation, log alteration, and misdirection \[ [4](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR4 "Majed, H., Noura, H.N., Chehab, A.: Overview of digital forensics and anti-forensics techniques. In 2020 8th International Symposium on Digital Forensics and Security (ISDFS) (pp. 1–5). IEEE. (2020), June"), [5](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR5 "Dahbur, K., Mohammad, B.: The anti-forensics challenge. In Proceedings of the 2011 International Conference on Intelligent Semantic Web-Services and Applications (pp. 1–7). (2011), April"), [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR6 "Hosgor, E.C.: Detection and mitigation of anti-forensics. Int. J. Comput. Sci. Inform. Secur. (IJCSIS), 18(12). (2020)")\]. Timestamp and file signature changes can cause evidence to be overlooked or dismissed \[ [5](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR5 "Dahbur, K., Mohammad, B.: The anti-forensics challenge. In Proceedings of the 2011 International Conference on Intelligent Semantic Web-Services and Applications (pp. 1–7). (2011), April")\], while complete log deletion can result in permanent loss of investigative data if not previously forwarded \[ [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR6 "Hosgor, E.C.: Detection and mitigation of anti-forensics. Int. J. Comput. Sci. Inform. Secur. (IJCSIS), 18(12). (2020)")\]. IP spoofing can conceal the true source of network traffic, obscuring attribution \[ [4](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR4 "Majed, H., Noura, H.N., Chehab, A.: Overview of digital forensics and anti-forensics techniques. In 2020 8th International Symposium on Digital Forensics and Security (ISDFS) (pp. 1–5). IEEE. (2020), June")\]. As these techniques can cause substantial investigative misdirection and irreversible data loss, the severity is rated High.

#### 5.1.4 Attacks against forensic tools

Attacks against forensic tools are well-documented in the literature, encompassing denial-of-service (DoS) through resource exhaustion (e.g., RAM and CPU) and anti–reverse engineering techniques such as compression bombs \[ [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR6 "Hosgor, E.C.: Detection and mitigation of anti-forensics. Int. J. Comput. Sci. Inform. Secur. (IJCSIS), 18(12). (2020)"), [22](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR22 "Tian, D., Ma, R., Jia, X., Hu, C.: A kernel rootkit detection approach based on virtualization and machine learning. IEEE Access. 7, 91657–91666 (2019)")\]. These methods have relatively low technical barriers — compression bomb creation and resource depletion can be achieved using publicly available scripts, utilities, or file generation techniques, requiring only basic technical knowledge. In addition \[ [4](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR4 "Majed, H., Noura, H.N., Chehab, A.: Overview of digital forensics and anti-forensics techniques. In 2020 8th International Symposium on Digital Forensics and Security (ISDFS) (pp. 1–5). IEEE. (2020), June")\], reports attempt to undermine the credibility of investigative parties, introducing reputational risks alongside operational disruption. The accessibility of these techniques, combined with their ability to remain unnoticed until tool performance degrades, contributes to their practical feasibility. Detection before disruption occurs is challenging, as the attacks often manifest only when analysis is already underway or system resources are critically low. Given the availability of methods, low implementation complexity, and potential stealth until impact is visible, the likelihood of occurrence is rated Moderate to High.

In terms of severity, such attacks can directly disrupt or halt forensic analysis — DoS attacks can render tools inoperable \[ [22](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR22 "Tian, D., Ma, R., Jia, X., Hu, C.: A kernel rootkit detection approach based on virtualization and machine learning. IEEE Access. 7, 91657–91666 (2019)")\] and anti–reverse engineering techniques can prevent access to or analysis of digital evidence \[ [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR6 "Hosgor, E.C.: Detection and mitigation of anti-forensics. Int. J. Comput. Sci. Inform. Secur. (IJCSIS), 18(12). (2020)")\]. Credibility attacks \[ [4](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR4 "Majed, H., Noura, H.N., Chehab, A.: Overview of digital forensics and anti-forensics techniques. In 2020 8th International Symposium on Digital Forensics and Security (ISDFS) (pp. 1–5). IEEE. (2020), June")\] further escalate the potential harm by undermining trust in investigative findings, which can compromise the integrity of legal proceedings. These impacts — ranging from significant delays to loss of investigative capability and erosion of evidentiary integrity — justify a high severity rating.

### 5.2 What problems do investigators face during forensic investigations?

#### 5.2.1 General challenges in DF

### Data acquisition

The proliferation of digital devices presents challenges in identifying, acquiring, storing, and analysing diverse sources of digital evidence, including computers, smartphones, cloud services, IoT devices, and wearables \[ [49](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR49 "Lillis, D., Becker, B., O’Sullivan, T., Scanlon, M.: Current challenges and future research areas for digital forensic investigation. arXiv preprint arXiv:1604.03850. (2016)")\].

### Evidence preservation

While evidence preservation is recognized as a phase in DF, there is a lack of guidance on securely preserving evidence, posing risks to its integrity \[ [9](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR9 "Singh, A., Ikuesan, R.A., Venter, H.: Secure storage model for digital forensic readiness. IEEE Access. 10, 19469–19480 (2022)")\].

### Legal issues

Investigators face challenges in collecting, preserving, and presenting evidence in court, navigating legal complexities surrounding digital evidence \[ [46](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR46 "Montasari, R., Hill, R.: Next-generation digital forensics: Challenges and future paradigms. In 2019 IEEE 12th International conference on global security, safety, and sustainability (ICGS3) (pp. 205–212). IEEE. (2019)"), [48](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR48 "Debbarma, R.: The legal framework and challenges in prosecuting cybercrimes including Hacking, identity Theft, and online fraud. Resmilitaris. 13(2), 7021–7048 (2023)")\].

### Privacy concerns

Ensuring the privacy and integrity of collected evidence presents ethical and procedural challenges for investigators.

### Human element risks

Human involvement introduces vulnerabilities in DF processes, making it the weakest link in investigations \[ [47](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR47 "Mothukur, A.R., Balla, A., Taylor, D.H., Sirimalla, S.T., Elleithy, K.: Investigation of countermeasures to anti-forensic methods. In 2019 IEEE Long Island Systems, Applications and Technology Conference (LISAT) (pp. 1–6). IEEE. (2019)")\].

### Tool limitations

Current forensic tools lack standardization and effectiveness, requiring practical testing and improvements to enhance reliability \[ [45](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR45 "Yaacoub, J.P.A., Noura, H.N., Salman, O., Chehab, A.: Digital forensics vs. Anti-digital forensics: Techniques, limitations, and recommendations. arXiv preprint arXiv:2103.17028. (2021)")\].

### Attack misuse

Advancements in technology are exploited by attackers, who use techniques like DDoS attacks to disrupt digital forensic investigations \[ [46](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR46 "Montasari, R., Hill, R.: Next-generation digital forensics: Challenges and future paradigms. In 2019 IEEE 12th International conference on global security, safety, and sustainability (ICGS3) (pp. 205–212). IEEE. (2019)")\].

Addressing these challenges requires a combination of technical expertise, procedural rigor, collaboration with relevant stakeholders, and adherence to legal and ethical standards to conduct thorough and effective forensic investigations.

#### 5.2.2 Factors across digital forensic investigation stages

Digital forensic investigations are influenced by several key factors at each stage of the process. In the collection and acquisition stage, factors such as evidence collection, data acquisition, privacy, and compliance with laws play a central role in ensuring that digital evidence is gathered accurately, without contamination, and in adherence to applicable legal requirements. The preservation and storage stage emphasizes factors including evidence preservation, maintaining a secure chain of custody, managing encryption, and safeguarding confidentiality, all of which are vital to maintaining the integrity and admissibility of digital evidence. During processing and analysis, the reliability of evidence analysis, limitations of available forensic tools, the growing volume of digital data, and the need to respect privacy emerge as critical considerations that shape the effectiveness of the investigative process. Finally, legal and ethical oversight involves factors such as compliance with evolving laws, protection of individual privacy, and addressing vulnerabilities linked to the human element, each of which significantly influences the credibility and ethical soundness of digital forensic investigations.

#### 5.2.3 Challenges in DF domains

### 5.3 What are the anti-forensics countermeasures, and how can we make them better?

Identifying anti-forensic countermeasures is important for ensuring that digital evidence remains reliable and usable in investigations. Attackers often use anti-forensic techniques to hide, alter, or destroy evidence, making it harder for investigators to find the truth. To ensure that digital evidence remains reliable and investigations remain effective, both organizational security managers and digital forensics practitioners need to adopt robust countermeasures against anti-forensic techniques and strengthen forensic readiness.

#### 5.3.1 Practical countermeasures for digital forensics practitioners

The countermeasures against anti-forensic (AF) techniques, as collected from various articles, focus on detecting tampering attempts, preserving evidence integrity, and improving recovery methods. These strategies help forensic practitioners stay ahead of evolving threats and maintain the credibility of digital investigations.

### Artifact wiping

To prevent attackers from wiping important evidence, organizations should keep regular data backups so that information is not lost forever \[ [4](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR4 "Majed, H., Noura, H.N., Chehab, A.: Overview of digital forensics and anti-forensics techniques. In 2020 8th International Symposium on Digital Forensics and Security (ISDFS) (pp. 1–5). IEEE. (2020), June")\]. It is also important to block the installation of artifact wiping tools by setting strong access controls \[ [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR6 "Hosgor, E.C.: Detection and mitigation of anti-forensics. Int. J. Comput. Sci. Inform. Secur. (IJCSIS), 18(12). (2020)")\]. Access to sensitive data should be limited to only trusted users to reduce the risk of data being wiped \[ [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR6 "Hosgor, E.C.: Detection and mitigation of anti-forensics. Int. J. Comput. Sci. Inform. Secur. (IJCSIS), 18(12). (2020)"), [69](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR69 "Parthy, P.P., Rajendran, G.: Identification and prevention of social engineering attacks on an enterprise. In 2019 International Carnahan Conference on Security Technology (ICCST) (pp. 1–5). IEEE. (2019)"), [70](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR70 "Arabia-Obedoza, M.R., Rodriguez, G., Johnston, A., Salahdine, F., Kaabouch, N.: Social engineering attacks a reconnaissance synthesis analysis. In 2020 11th IEEE Annual Ubiquitous Computing, Electronics & Mobile Communication Conference (UEMCON) (pp. 0843–0848). IEEE. (2020)")\].

### Data hiding

To find hidden data, forensic experts can use tools like FTK Imager, Bulk Extractor, and The Sleuth Kit (TSK) to scan the file system. Steganography tools such as Gargoyle and StegDetect help uncover information hidden inside images or other files \[ [4](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR4 "Majed, H., Noura, H.N., Chehab, A.: Overview of digital forensics and anti-forensics techniques. In 2020 8th International Symposium on Digital Forensics and Security (ISDFS) (pp. 1–5). IEEE. (2020), June")\]. Using cryptographic hashing can also check if any data has been secretly changed or hidden \[ [7](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR7 "Al-Mousa, M.R., Sweerky, N.A., Samara, G., Alghanim, M., Hussein, A.S.I., Qadoumi, B.: General countermeasures of Anti-Forensics categories. In: 2021 Global Congress on Electrical Engineering (GC-ElecEng), pp. 5–10. IEEE (2021)")\].

### Encryption

When encryption is used to hide data, memory dumping and brute force detection methods can help find it \[ [4](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR4 "Majed, H., Noura, H.N., Chehab, A.: Overview of digital forensics and anti-forensics techniques. In 2020 8th International Symposium on Digital Forensics and Security (ISDFS) (pp. 1–5). IEEE. (2020), June")\]. Tools like TSK Autopsy are useful for spotting encryption software like VeraCrypt on a device \[ [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR6 "Hosgor, E.C.: Detection and mitigation of anti-forensics. Int. J. Comput. Sci. Inform. Secur. (IJCSIS), 18(12). (2020)")\].

### Network-based data hiding

To stop hidden data transfers over networks, organizations can use firewalls to block VPNs and make sure data is sent securely using SSL \[ [4](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR4 "Majed, H., Noura, H.N., Chehab, A.: Overview of digital forensics and anti-forensics techniques. In 2020 8th International Symposium on Digital Forensics and Security (ISDFS) (pp. 1–5). IEEE. (2020), June")\]. Network monitoring tools like Wireshark can help check network traffic for any signs of hidden data \[ [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR6 "Hosgor, E.C.: Detection and mitigation of anti-forensics. Int. J. Comput. Sci. Inform. Secur. (IJCSIS), 18(12). (2020)")\].

### Log manipulation

To protect logs from tampering, they should be stored safely and backed up often. Using endpoint security can help spot unauthorized access \[ [4](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR4 "Majed, H., Noura, H.N., Chehab, A.: Overview of digital forensics and anti-forensics techniques. In 2020 8th International Symposium on Digital Forensics and Security (ISDFS) (pp. 1–5). IEEE. (2020), June")\]. SIEM (Security Information and Event Management) systems help collect and analyse logs in one place \[ [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR6 "Hosgor, E.C.: Detection and mitigation of anti-forensics. Int. J. Comput. Sci. Inform. Secur. (IJCSIS), 18(12). (2020)")\].

### Attacks against forensic tools and processes

To protect forensic tools, tools like Burn Eye can detect hidden program packers. It is also important to update and patch systems to fix any security holes \[ [4](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR4 "Majed, H., Noura, H.N., Chehab, A.: Overview of digital forensics and anti-forensics techniques. In 2020 8th International Symposium on Digital Forensics and Security (ISDFS) (pp. 1–5). IEEE. (2020), June")\]. Other helpful steps include lowering investigators’ workloads \[ [7](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR7 "Al-Mousa, M.R., Sweerky, N.A., Samara, G., Alghanim, M., Hussein, A.S.I., Qadoumi, B.: General countermeasures of Anti-Forensics categories. In: 2021 Global Congress on Electrical Engineering (GC-ElecEng), pp. 5–10. IEEE (2021)")\], providing training and updates at conferences, and giving them modern hardware and software \[ [7](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR7 "Al-Mousa, M.R., Sweerky, N.A., Samara, G., Alghanim, M., Hussein, A.S.I., Qadoumi, B.: General countermeasures of Anti-Forensics categories. In: 2021 Global Congress on Electrical Engineering (GC-ElecEng), pp. 5–10. IEEE (2021)"), [69](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR69 "Parthy, P.P., Rajendran, G.: Identification and prevention of social engineering attacks on an enterprise. In 2019 International Carnahan Conference on Security Technology (ICCST) (pp. 1–5). IEEE. (2019)")\]. Using smart decompression libraries can protect against malicious compression tricks like compression bombs \[ [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR6 "Hosgor, E.C.: Detection and mitigation of anti-forensics. Int. J. Comput. Sci. Inform. Secur. (IJCSIS), 18(12). (2020)")\].

Anti-forensics countermeasures are techniques and strategies used by cybercriminals to evade detection and hinder digital forensic investigations. These countermeasures aim to disrupt or undermine forensic analysis, making it more challenging for investigators to collect, preserve, and analyse digital evidence. Forensic investigators should improve their ability to detect, mitigate, and counteract anti-forensic techniques employed by cybercriminals, thereby enhancing the effectiveness of digital forensic investigations.

#### 5.3.2 Technical and process-based forensic readiness measures

Here are some recommendations collected from different articles to make digital forensics stronger and more effective. These suggestions help investigators find hidden evidence, protect data, and stay prepared against new tricks used by attackers. Using these best practices can improve the reliability of digital investigations:

### Regular database updates and hybrid analysis

Authors recommend updating databases regularly and adopting hybrid analysis techniques combining static and dynamic analysis for more effective cybercrime investigation methods \[ [12](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR12 "Al-Asli, M., Ghaleb, T.A.: Review of signature-based techniques in antivirus products. In 2019 International Conference on Computer and Information Sciences (ICCIS) (pp. 1–6). IEEE. (2019)")\].

### On-site forensic acquisitions

Strategies for conducting on-site forensic acquisitions of live computer systems are suggested to enhance the likelihood of obtaining digital evidence in an unencrypted state or capturing encryption keys/passphrases \[ [50](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR50 "Casey, E., Fellows, G., Geiger, M., Stellatos, G.: The growing impact of full disk encryption on digital forensics. Digit. Invest. 8(2), 129–134 (2011)")\].

### Validation of forensic processes

To overcome anti-forensic methods, authors propose validating each phase of the digital forensics process and evaluating the results before proceeding to the next step \[ [2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR2 "Mothi, D., Janicke, H., Wagner, I.: A novel principle to validate digital forensic models. Forensic Sci. International: Digit. Invest. 33, 200904 (2020)")\].

### Implement proactive forensic methods

Authors advocate for the implementation of proactive forensic methods to prevent attacks beforehand rather than reacting to them after the damage has been done \[ [10](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR10 "Tanner, A., Dancer, F.C., Hall, J., Parker, N., Bishop, R., McBride, T.: The Need for Proactive Digital Forensics in Addressing Critical Infrastructure Cyber Attacks. In 2022 International Conference on Computational Science and Computational Intelligence (CSCI) (pp. 976–982). IEEE. (2022)")\].

### Data integrity preservation

Emphasizing the importance of data integrity \[ [72](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR72 "Pallivalappil, A.S., Jagadeesha, S.N.: Procedures for Digital Forensics and Incident Response on Including Data Integrity Constraints on Solid-State Drives (SSD)-A Literature Review. Int. J. Case Stud. Bus. IT Educ. (IJCSBE). 6(1), 328–350 (2022).")\], recommends making copies of collected data and avoiding working on the main data source to mitigate the risk of manipulation \[ [72](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR72 "Pallivalappil, A.S., Jagadeesha, S.N.: Procedures for Digital Forensics and Incident Response on Including Data Integrity Constraints on Solid-State Drives (SSD)-A Literature Review. Int. J. Case Stud. Bus. IT Educ. (IJCSBE). 6(1), 328–350 (2022).")\].

### Regular log comparison

Daily comparison of logs saved in different locations is recommended to identify any modifications or confirm that there are none, enhancing data integrity and security \[ [8](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR8 "Hammoud, A.H.: Efficient Countermeasures against Anti-Forensics Techniques. (2022)")\].

### Monitoring important files and folders

Tracking or monitoring important files and folders can improve the effectiveness of File Integrity Monitoring (FIM) tools by focusing on critical areas \[ [59](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR59 "Mane, P.P.D., Jadhav, A., Sathe, O., Lingade, V., Nagane, A., Jagdale: P. File Integrity Monitoring")\].

### Utilization of watermarking techniques

Watermarking is suggested as a crucial method to enhance the security of digital information, providing protection against tampering, access control, ownership authentication, and non-repudiation \[ [56](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR56 "Singh, A.K.: Data hiding: Current trends, innovation, and potential challenges. ACM Trans. Multimedia Comput. Commun. Appl. (TOMM). 16(3s), 1–16 (2020)")\].

### 5.4 Broader organizational security and management measures

Apart from the above, the following actions strengthen overall readiness and support forensic efforts:

### Invest in training and education

Provide comprehensive training and education programs for forensic investigators and cybersecurity professionals to ensure they have the necessary skills and expertise to conduct effective digital forensic investigations \[ [7](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR7 "Al-Mousa, M.R., Sweerky, N.A., Samara, G., Alghanim, M., Hussein, A.S.I., Qadoumi, B.: General countermeasures of Anti-Forensics categories. In: 2021 Global Congress on Electrical Engineering (GC-ElecEng), pp. 5–10. IEEE (2021)"), [69](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR69 "Parthy, P.P., Rajendran, G.: Identification and prevention of social engineering attacks on an enterprise. In 2019 International Carnahan Conference on Security Technology (ICCST) (pp. 1–5). IEEE. (2019)")\]. Offer hands-on training, certifications, and continuing education opportunities to keep investigators up to date with the latest tools, techniques, and best practices in digital forensics. Not only your employees, but your customers and other external stakeholders need to be offered education and training.

### Develop standard operating procedures (SOPs)

Establish clear and standardized procedures for conducting digital forensic investigations, including evidence collection, preservation, analysis, and reporting \[ [5](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR5 "Dahbur, K., Mohammad, B.: The anti-forensics challenge. In Proceedings of the 2011 International Conference on Intelligent Semantic Web-Services and Applications (pp. 1–7). (2011), April")\]. SOPs should adhere to legal and regulatory requirements, industry standards, and best practices in digital forensics to ensure consistency and integrity throughout the investigation process.

### Deploy advanced forensic tools and technologies

Invest in state-of-the-art forensic tools and technologies to enhance the capabilities of forensic investigators. This includes forensic imaging software, memory analysis tools, mobile device forensics solutions, and network forensic appliances that can assist in collecting, analysing, and preserving digital evidence from diverse sources.

### Implement continuous monitoring solutions

Deploy continuous monitoring solutions, such as Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) systems and Endpoint Detection and Response (EDR) platforms, to detect and respond to security incidents in real-time \[ [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10207-025-01131-y#ref-CR6 "Hosgor, E.C.: Detection and mitigation of anti-forensics. Int. J. Comput. Sci. Inform. Secur. (IJCSIS), 18(12). (2020)")\]. These solutions can help forensic investigators identify suspicious activities, conduct timely investigations, and mitigate cyber threats before they escalate. It is better to automate monitoring systems that provide alerts for responses and incident actions.

### Establish digital evidence management systems

Implement robust digital evidence management systems to securely store, catalogue, and manage digital evidence throughout its lifecycle. These systems should provide features for the evidence tracking chain of custody management, audit logging, and access control to ensure the integrity and admissibility of digital evidence in legal proceedings.

## 6 Contribution to new knowledge

In this sub-section, the implications of the findings are explored, along with the identified knowledge gaps. A thorough review of the literature reveals several crucial areas requiring further research and exploration. The goal here is to underscore these gaps in understanding and suggest potential avenues for future investigation.

### Re-evaluation of AF techniques categorization

The literature review revealed a common categorization of AF techniques into artifact wiping, data hiding, trail obfuscation, and attacks against forensic tools. They often fail to explain how these attacks negatively affect the DF investigation process. Therefore, it is needed to introduce a new categorization framework that illustrates how attacks target each stage of the DF process. By doing so, investigators can remain vigilant throughout each phase of the investigation to detect and mitigate potential attacks or malicious activities effectively.

### Identification of undisclosed attacks

The review highlights the existence of many overlooked attacks that significantly impact the DF process but are not extensively discussed in AF-related literature. It is imperative to bring attention to these lesser-known attacks to equip investigators and researchers with a comprehensive understanding of potential threats. This knowledge will enable the development of advanced and robust countermeasures to enhance cybersecurity practices.

### Need for supplementary countermeasures

The literature review also reveals that existing AF countermeasure papers offer only a limited number of countermeasures, primarily focusing on basic strategies or specific tools. Supplementary countermeasures, beyond those commonly proposed, should be explored and included in future research to enhance the effectiveness of DF investigations. By conducting further research on various cyber threats impacting DF, researchers can identify innovative strategies to mitigate the adverse effects of AF techniques effectively.

### Importance of practical validation

Many AF countermeasure proposals lack practical validation, such as tool testing or real-world experimentation. To ensure the reliability and effectiveness of suggested countermeasures, the countermeasures should be applied in real-world applications and collect empirical evidence of their efficacy. Future research should prioritize practical validation through rigorous testing and experimentation to validate the proposed countermeasures in real-world scenarios. This approach will enhance confidence in the effectiveness of countermeasures and their applicability in practical DF settings.

### Reliability issues with current tools

The existing literature indicates a significant knowledge gap regarding the reliability and effectiveness of current digital forensics (DF) tools against anti-forensic (AF) attacks. Several studies have demonstrated that current DF tools remain vulnerable to various AF techniques, highlighting the need for more robust and reliable tools. Additionally, there is a limited understanding of the limitations of various analysis techniques employed in DF processes. Addressing these knowledge gaps is crucial for developing more resilient DF strategies and tools capable of effectively countering AF attacks in cybersecurity investigations.

Addressing these knowledge gaps will contribute to advancing the field of digital forensics by improving our understanding of AF techniques, identifying potential threats, expanding the range of countermeasures, and validating their effectiveness in real-world contexts.

## 8 Conclusion

The comprehensive literature review conducted sheds light on the complex landscape of digital forensics (DF) and the emerging challenges posed by anti-forensic (AF) techniques. Through an analysis of 75 articles spanning various domains within DF and AF, several key findings and insights have emerged. Firstly, the importance of understanding digital forensics as a prerequisite to comprehending anti-forensic tactics is evident. The review elucidates the significance of DF in investigating cybercrimes and the crucial role it plays in preserving digital evidence for legal proceedings. However, the emergence of AF techniques threatens the integrity and effectiveness of DF processes, underscoring the need for robust countermeasures.

The categorization of AF techniques into artifact wiping, data hiding, trail obfuscation, and attacks against forensic tools provides a structured framework for understanding the methods employed by cybercriminals. However, the review also highlights the limitations of current DF techniques against AF, as evidenced by experiments and testing. Concerns regarding the reliability of DF tools, analysis methods, and the trustworthiness of memory forensic techniques raise questions about their efficacy in combating cyber threats. Furthermore, the review identifies gaps in existing research, including the necessity for novel categories to elucidate precisely how AF disrupts the DF process. Additionally, the lack of comprehensive testing and experimentation to validate proposed countermeasures underscores the importance of empirical evidence in enhancing DF capabilities.

Countering anti-forensic tactics in cybercrime investigations demands a comprehensive and dynamic approach that integrates technical innovation, procedural rigor, legal frameworks, and collaborative efforts across stakeholders. By developing specialized tools and techniques, enhancing digital evidence collection and analysis capabilities, strengthening incident response and threat hunting capabilities, implementing forensic readiness programs, fostering collaboration and information sharing, addressing legal and regulatory challenges, and providing continuous training and education, organizations and law enforcement agencies can effectively mitigate the impact of anti-forensic tactics employed by cybercriminals. Through these concerted efforts, the resilience and efficacy of cybercrime investigations can be significantly enhanced, ensuring the preservation of digital evidence integrity and the pursuit of justice in the face of evolving cyber threats.

In conclusion, DF tools and methods have improved in fighting cybercrimes. The evolving nature of AF techniques necessitates continuous innovation and improvement. Future research should focus on addressing the identified limitations, developing advanced DF technologies, and establishing standardized frameworks to counter emerging cyber threats effectively. Only through collaborative efforts and interdisciplinary approaches can we strengthen the resilience of digital environments and uphold the integrity of forensic investigations in the face of evolving cyber threats.

To address shortcomings in the current practices of countering anti-forensic tactics in cybercrime investigations, it’s crucial to adopt a proactive and adaptive approach that addresses key deficiencies and incorporates best practices from the field. We recommend that organisations invest in research and development initiatives focused on identifying and mitigating emerging anti-forensic tactics and techniques. They also collaborate with academia, industry partners, and government agencies to conduct research, share insights, and develop innovative solutions to counteract evolving cyber threats effectively.
